The title is not a command, but a desperate plea. Because soon, those AI[^1] tokens you're maxxing will be too expensive to rely on.

If you use AI to brainstorm, program, copy edit, whatever it may be, I am begging you: please keep your skills sharp.

This is a selfish request. Odds are, if you're reading this, you work or maintain something I rely on. Whether you're the sole writer of a newsletter or one of many engineers maintaining infrastructure. When token prices increase, it is imperative that you can continue your craft without the use of AI.

Historically, it is not wise to predict technology's future. But a recent {{< tracked-anchor href="https://blog.jim-nielsen.com/2026/blogging-stating-the-obvious/" text="Jim Nielson blog post" >}} put it perfectly: *sometimes blogging is stating the obvious.*

I'd assume it obvious that tech services get more expensive over time, but it's worth saying anyway. 

The Loss Leader business model is ubiquitous. Netflix started with dirt cheap prices to convince people to cut the cord. {{< tracked-anchor href="https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/streaming-inflation-raising-prices-cable-1236691404/" text="Now streamers cost more than cable" >}}. Social media used to be free. Now verification is a credit card tap away. {{< tracked-anchor href="https://www.forrester.com/blogs/anthropics-pricing-shift-puts-ai-consumption-risk-back-on-customers/" text="Anthropic used to offer API discounts but no longer does" >}}. {{< tracked-anchor href="https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt" text="ChatGPT striped features from its free plan and plans to add advertisements even to paid tiers" >}}. It will only get worse. Unsurprising since {{< tracked-anchor href="https://fortune.com/2026/06/16/openai-financials-leaked-losses-revenue-profit/" text="Open AI spends $1.61 for every dollar it earns" >}}.

AI degrading your skills may be less obvious, but it is a clear conclusion if you look at the recent past. I.e, {{< tracked-anchor href="https://www.nature.com/articles/531573a.epdf?sharing_token=JVw5julzmzooFzIfgH5Nl9RgN0jAjWel9jnR3ZoTv0NWA3IrsxVrqQgY5PEUZd7PVhZVXA-Rh81Fis2PNrS7yzcJq0v_mHeebJSCgCU_zcy-4CuX6mpoNcpRXHY0Wr5yHsL6aeEsBGNN-8nb1HiKraM75OZH_pgPcSgyZexrfQZMqWoIdqt1Sf8q0xQxoCBOQpNbWAHExjSRcTk_NWxlxaXxO3fuUJSwT0rObrQlXfE%3D&tracking_referrer=www.scientificamerican.com" text="GPS making us worse at spatial navigation" >}}. Some of you reading this might not know a world without a map in your pocket. As someone who does, I can confidently say: AI will make you worse at whatever you use it for. {{< tracked-anchor href="https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/is-ai-ruining-our-skills-early-results-are-in-and-theyre-not-good/" text="Research already shows this" >}}. But not just our skills. {{< tracked-anchor href="https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jun/19/chatbots-critical-thinking-skills/" text="Chatbots degrade our very ability to think critically" >}}.

I cannot tell you *when* AI tokens will become prohibitively expensive. But I can guarantee, eventually you will be priced out of the AI model you currently use. When that day comes, for all our sake, I hope you kept you skills sharp.
